# What published corrections require of an identity index

Anno's grounded types answer three useful questions. A `Signal` anchors an occurrence, a `Track` groups signals in a document, and an `Identity` can give a track a catalog binding. A cross-document membership publication can report which tracks its resolver groups in that edition. A different question arises when a reader asks *who made this work?* A publisher can revise its answer even when every document, signal and catalog binding stays the same. Below are public cases in which the difference matters. [Grounded types](../../crates/anno/src/core/grounded/mod.rs) · [Membership publisher](../../crates/anno/src/coalesce/memberships.rs)

## One painting, two publishers

In October 2022 the National Gallery of Art announced that its examination of *Girl with a Flute* pointed to an unidentified associate of Vermeer. Its public collection record uses the broader artist heading *Dutch 17th Century*. The following month the Rijksmuseum listed the very same painting among the Vermeers planned for its 2023 exhibition and named Washington as the lender. One can identify the object with considerable confidence while remaining uncertain about the painter. A reader who asks for the lender’s attribution should be able to retrieve it after the later exhibition announcement. [NGA research announcement](https://www.nga.gov/press/new-findings-national-gallery-art-suggest-existence-studio-vermeer) · [NGA object record](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/1237-girl-flute) · [Rijksmuseum exhibition list](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/press/press-releases/rijksmuseum-announces-list-of-vermeer-paintings)

A credit can therefore have a definite source and an uncertain subject. Anno can identify the track and qualified catalog reference when one exists. The application can record who made a particular assignment, for which work, on which evidence and in which release. It can leave the maker unidentified. A model score can rank candidate painters, but the score supplies neither the publisher's decision nor permission to show it. Linked Art's attribute assignment provides a working precedent for representing the person or group making a judgment and the property being assigned. [Linked Art assertions](https://linked.art/model/assertion/) · [Production and attribution](https://linked.art/model/object/production/)

## One predecessor, several people

Four public Library of Congress authority records identify the same former undifferentiated record, `n82004044`. Each cites different supporting works. One differentiated record can still display an unqualified name. The cataloging instructions direct staff to move evidence for the individual they can distinguish. A redirect from the old ID to the first or last successor would put other people's works under that heading. An index can offer all known successors as leads, preserve the source citation, and wait for evidence before reassigning an individual mention. The four records establish a one-to-many example. They do not enumerate every historical member of the former record. [Cataloging instructions, §008/32](https://www.loc.gov/aba/publications/FreeDCM/DCM/Z01.pdf) · [First record](https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/no2014038610) · [Second](https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/no2014025694) · [Third](https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/n2014015173) · [Fourth](https://id.loc.gov/authorities/names/nb2008012105)

Catalog time also has several meanings. Emory archivists documented revising a folder heading. The current finding aid confirms the revised display for Box 1, Folder 39. The years printed there describe material in the folder. They supply no dates for when the person used a name or when the edit took effect. In a separate Yale case, archivists retained an engraving's transcribed title and added context in the Palmquist finding aid. A reader retrieving the artist's wording needs the source occurrence. A reader asking for the archive's current description needs the relevant edition. [Emory archivists' account](https://exhibits.libraries.emory.edu/the-work-is-never-finished/reparative-description/) · [Emory folder](https://archives.libraries.emory.edu/repositories/6/archival_objects/473619) · [Yale project account](https://guides.library.yale.edu/c.php?g=1140330&p=9165488&t=133180) · [Yale finding aid](https://archives.yale.edu/repositories/11/resources/1351)

## What a correction changes, and who can see it

In COPE case 18-07 an editor faced two issues on one paper. A surname needed correction and authors disagreed about whether the person should be credited at all. The journal reported that it intended to issue an erratum for the name and an expression of concern about authorship. The case is marked ongoing. A single accepted/disputed flag for the whole identity would make it impossible to record both decisions accurately. [Editorial case](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/case/inconclusive-institutional-investigation-authorship-dispute)

Publication practices also set different visibility requirements. APS offers a confidential route for updating an author's name and describes how prior-name search is handled on its site. ACS reported in November 2022 that it had updated approximately 400 articles for nearly 100 researchers in two years. The totals describe requests the operator fulfilled. The underlying population of researchers is unknown. A public audit diff, alias lookup or autocomplete result can reveal an association even after the article display has changed. A consumer needs a decision for each surface it serves. Retention and access to historical evidence also require a policy. A catalog that preserves a transcribed title and a publisher that removes an earlier byline from search can each be carrying out its mandate. [APS policy](https://journals.aps.org/aps-name-change-policy) · [ACS implementation report](https://axial.acs.org/publishing/acs-publications-name-change-policy-update) · [COPE confidentiality guidance](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/cope-position/handling-requests-author-name-changes)

## Where propagation stops

A September 2024 *Scientific Reports* correction added an author omitted from a July article and amended the contribution statement. The article retained its DOI. The notice has a separate DOI. We checked both Crossref records. The notice points to the article via `update-to`, and the article points back via `updated-by`. Both `relation` objects were empty. A reader using only `relation` would miss this correction. This verifies one registry's state at inspection. It supplies no propagation time or acknowledgement from any other index. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-66974-2) · [Correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-72303-4) · [Article registry record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41598-024-66974-2) · [Notice registry record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1038/s41598-024-72303-4)

George and colleagues describe a concrete limit in library discovery. At SUNY, a proposal to change a subject heading was approved in January 2020. Changes to the Primo VE display followed in June and changes to managed MARC records in August. The library's rules could not alter records from the Central Discovery Index. A later national replacement split a heading in two. These cases concern subjects, but they reveal a common indexing problem. Source acceptance, delivery of metadata, and a verified result at a named reader are different events. Record which one completed. [Practitioner study](https://journals.ala.org/index.php/lrts/article/view/7467/10545) · [Library of Congress decision](https://www.loc.gov/aba/pcc/saco/cpsoed/psd-211115.html)

## The design decision

Keep the grounded progression. Give downstream readers explicit inputs for a work, a qualified target, the relationship being asserted, the issuer, evidence and visibility at a revision. Two independent sources can support the same credit. On withdrawal a reader recomputes which support the viewer can still use. A printed credit remains answerable from a visible signal even when the person-specific lookup has no remaining support.

The synthetic replay tests full rebuilds and incremental readers against the same hand-authored answers. A native membership checkpoint covers membership data. To cover a changing credit policy as well, a Rust caller can publish credit rows in a separate `IndexSnapshot` and pin both checkpoints in an `IndexEdition`. The SQLite `anno query edition` receiver currently accepts its own query-publication format. An application using the generic Rust streams must arrange its storage, writer authentication and authorization. [Replay and scope](../../verification/index_consumers/credits/README.md) · [Rust regression](../../crates/anno/tests/membership_publication.rs) · [CLI receipt format](../../crates/anno-cli/src/cli/commands/query/edition/receipt.rs)

The next design test is end to end at a second reader. Deliver a withdrawal, restart that reader with a populated index, and check the result page, autocomplete, counts and historical lookup through its public interface. Record which answers changed and which evidence still explains them. The three fixtures establish correctness conditions for those experiments. They do not measure how often real catalogers encounter these cases or how a model performs on unseen records.
