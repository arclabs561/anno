# Which joint predictions help a reviewer?

A useful joint decision has a visible outcome. For someone checking dialogue it might be “the speaker and one listener are both correct.” For someone checking a fact it might be “both entities and the directed relation are correct.” A model can improve either outcome while making one component worse. We compared the components and the joint outcome wherever the corpus supplied both.

| User decision | What changed in this run | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Approve a speaker and an addressee | Across 36,446 PDNC quotations, a soft penalty for predicting the same person in both roles raised both-correct accuracy from 19.03% to 29.70%, with gains in all 28 novels. Addressee-only accuracy fell from 51.02% to 49.71%. Requiring the complete audience and speaker yielded 5,547 correct, up from 3,427. | The joint suggestion is worth testing with predicted rosters and boundaries. Compare Sheaf against this inexpensive penalty, particularly on implicit dialogue. [PDNC protocol](pdnc/README.md) |
| Approve a catalog page or directed fact | On 100 DWIE news documents, reranking with known types, aliases and facts raised link F1 from 82.24 to 83.61 and relation F1 from 47.16 to 48.44 over the shared-page baseline. Incorrect identity pairs rose from 182 to 216. Returned relations came from training lookup. | Offer page and fact suggestions independently from cluster acceptance. Test genuinely novel facts before an extraction claim. [DWIE protocol](dwie/README.md) |
| Accept a typed scientific relation | In 100 SciERC test abstracts, independent local scores gave 11.84 typed-relation F1, feeding predicted types forward gave 20.24, and letting edges revise types gave 19.44. Typed coreference pair F1 likewise went from 34.16 under the pipeline to 32.55 with feedback. | The cheaper one-way pipeline is the control to beat on scientific text. These use supplied spans. [SciERC protocol](scierc/README.md) |
| Select a bridge antecedent and relation | On 558 GUMBridge test queries, joint count-based decoding recovered 69 correct target/subtype pairs versus 66 in sequence, while dropping correct targets from 161 to 147. On 379 out-of-domain queries, joint decoding got 28 correct pairs versus 29. | The small test gain failed to transfer across genres. Compare containment with symmetric scores and keep target recovery visible. English DiscoTrack reuses GUM data. [Bridging and DiscoTrack](bridging/README.md) |

Among eight recent entities, 50/74 groups had all members available, but simply choosing the two most recent got 9/74 exactly right. Joint routing and sequential lexical decoding tied on correct offers. | The output needs a segment or a set. Offer source selections and evaluate group completeness. [Dialogue protocol](deixis/README.md) |

## Transfer and evidence retrieval at scale

**WikiCoref reversed the previous different-text result.** We froze the PreCo history threshold and the original MiniLM feature and score recipe, with no WikiCoref tuning. Across 30 articles, 6,991 supplied mentions produced 4,930 different-text proposals. At 974 reviews, exemplars gave 490 incorrect visible pairs and 600 wrong or mixed clusters. Adding midpoint scores gave 489 and 598. Adding boxes gave 497 and 649. The paired interval for box-induced pair-error reduction was −2.29 to +0.90 percentage points. Cluster errors rose by 5.03 points (95% interval +3.60 to +6.58). Together with the earlier LitBank and PreCo outcomes, this rules out a general-purpose recommendation for this fixed box reranker. [Frozen transfer protocol](wikicoref/README.md)

**CorefUD across languages.** In the public reformatted development shard (19 languages, 1,751 documents), recency recovered 24,232 of 91,178 returning mentions, exact-text priority 43,358, and additionally matching an annotated head 50,336. On the subset where the gold entity had never used the current form, exact-text priority selected the wrong competing entity often enough to lose 3,742 correct suggestions relative to recency. That subset is identifiable only in hindsight. Test predicted heads and show the source evidence before a merge. The shard has 29 corpus identifiers and includes material shared with earlier English experiments. [Multilingual protocol](multilingual/README.md)

**BookCoref.** Across three full gold novels, a 24-character memory retained the correct person for 615/654 returns after at least 1,024 tokens. Recency recovered only 9 of those 654 in its top four and frequency recovered 121. The same recency rule recovered 20,873/22,987 across returns of every length. This is a case for improving long-gap ranking among retained characters. The experiment supplies gold character histories. Pride and Prejudice contributed 484 of the long-gap queries. [Book-scale protocol](../bookscale/README.md)

**Directional bridge geometry.** With 86 fitted parameters per arm and three seeds, ordered boxes recovered 155–156/558 GUM test antecedents. Symmetric boxes recovered 149–150, reversing the learned containment direction recovered 143–145, role-specific points recovered 159, and simple ordered counts recovered 161. Direction was useful within the box model. The point and count controls still ranked more targets correctly. The CPU implementation used NumPy/SciPy. [Geometry protocol](bridging/README.md)

**Witnessed graph queries.** Intersecting a two-hop director/film/cast path with an observed profession on FB15k-237 gave 7,795 answers witnessed in the training graph across 2,192 queries. Limiting traversal to one, three or eight films recovered 3,745, 6,182 and 7,605 respectively. The official test split supplied witnesses for 2,028 answers beyond the train-plus-validation snapshot. The published Heyting 0.17.0 CPU backend reproduced all 7,795 training answers across every query against an independent set join. The pruning comparison used Python. [Typed-query protocol](https://github.com/arclabs561/heyting/tree/main/docs/experiments/typed-film-query)

## What should change in the crates

* **Anno** can represent one displayed quote with two independently correctable role assignments. A group antecedent needs several source spans, and discourse deixis needs a span over an utterance. A single global cluster-accept action loses these distinctions.
* **Subsume and Tuplet** belong in matched comparisons for type or bridge compatibility. Use train-only type information, equal parameter counts, an ordered point model, a symmetric box, and an argument-swap control. Validate direction on real edge labels. On observed UFET type cooccurrences, comparing the two empirical conditional probabilities reproduced the marginal-frequency ordering exactly.
* **Heyting** can preserve witnesses for conjunctions and paths. Distinguish “no observed witness,” “query budget exhausted,” and an explicit contradictory fact. Only the last supplies negative evidence.
* **Ricci and Sheaf** have distinct tests. For Ricci, ask whether an observable relation graph improves candidate retrieval above the DWIE alias/type/fact lookup. For Sheaf, first measure disagreement between attribution views and compare resolution against the simple PDNC role penalty. Keep both roles' error counts visible.

These experiments compare simple decoders and retrieval controls. The PDNC run supplied quote boundaries and a full roster. DWIE and SciERC supplied mention boundaries. The dialogue diagnostic supplied previous entity identities. The event, catalog and query audits test reachability under their documented inputs. None measures a person's time, and none establishes end-to-end Anno accuracy. Treat the exploratory comparisons as hypotheses for a held-out workload with predicted inputs. Corpus sources remain upstream. The subdirectories contain executable experiments and aggregate receipts.
